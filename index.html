The client for worlds that are web pages.

A world is a document at a URL. WebVerse is the page that opens it: on a phone, a laptop or a headset's browser, with nothing to install. Every published world's link opens WebVerse already pointed at that world.

Open any world by its address

Paste the address of a world's index.veml. The link below opens WebVerse already pointed at it. The world can be served from anywhere: your own domain, GitHub Pages, a bucket.

The link that opens it:

https://worlds.worldhub.me/webverse/index.html#world-url=…

If you host worlds, link to it

Give visitors one link. It is the client's address with the world's address after #world-url=, URL-encoded:

<a href="https://worlds.worldhub.me/webverse/index.html
   #world-url=https%3A%2F%2Fyour.site%2Froom%2Findex.veml">
  Walk in
</a>

A world published from Blender already comes with such a link.

Read the world you are in

Add /index.veml to any published world's address and the whole document opens as text. It is short, and every line means something you can see.

What it does

Opens any world by its address

and follows doors from one world into another.

Puts visitors in the same room

Others appear, and move, as they move.

Runs a world's scripts in a sandbox

so a world can do things and cannot reach outside.

Shows something walkable first

and fills in the detail while you are already there.

Getting around

Press H in any world to see these again.

Look

Click in the world to look around with the mouse. Esc gives the mouse back.

Move

W A S D to move, Space to jump. F to fly where the world allows it: Space up, Shift down.

On a phone or tablet

The left stick moves, the right stick looks. Jump, Chat and Fly sit above them.

What it is not

Not an app store, not an account, not a place worlds live. The world is files at its own address; WebVerse is how a browser shows them. A world at your own address does not depend on this site. Today the client page it opens in is served from worlds.worldhub.me; the public release lets anyone host that too.

Open source

WebVerse and the engine under it, StraightFour, are MIT-licensed. The public release of the current generation is being prepared; the earlier generation is at github.com/Five-Squared-Interactive.
